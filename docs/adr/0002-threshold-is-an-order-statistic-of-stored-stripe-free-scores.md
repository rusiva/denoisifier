# The Threshold is an order statistic of stored per-frame Stripe-free scores at full frame

The package ships a Calibration Set's per-frame Stripe Scores, not one threshold: 300 generated Stripe-free 400x1600 frames at typical settings (scorched mottle, about 100 photons at the peak, EMCCD noise, 30 spike events per frame, unclipped), scored by the full pipeline. The Threshold at a requested false-alarm rate r is the k-th largest stored score, k = floor(r (n + 1)); at n = 300 the default 1% is rank 3 and 5% is rank 15. We chose this over an analytic noise model (ADR 0001), over one stored number (any rate would need a new run, and a changed score could not be traced to a frame), and over per-shape tables or an area-corrected score (more to calibrate and justify than the one frame size real users record). It is hard to reverse once users hold calibration files of this format, and the full-frame choice and its scope are surprising without the reason.

## Consequences

- **Scope of the rate claim.** The default 1% holds for unclipped 400x1600 frames at typical settings. On crops and smaller frames the rate is expected to be lower (smaller light area), but a generated small frame is not a crop of a full one, so this is an expectation, not a guarantee. Frames with a larger light area (for example 640x1024) and binned frames are not covered. Clipped frames were measured once at ticket 07's close, not against a tolerance: 0 False Alarms in 200 Stripe-free 400x1600 frames clipped at 0.4 of their clean maximum (exact 95% interval 0-1.8%; highest score 92.6), so clipping at that level gave no sign of a raised rate.
- **Precision.** At rank 3 of 300 the shipped "1%" is only known to lie within about 0.2-2.4% (95%); the smallest requestable rate is 1/(n + 1) = 0.33%. About a tenth of Stripe-free frames score exactly 0, so a requested rate above the share of positive scores gives a threshold of 0 or a tie, and the realised rate is then below the request.
- **Spikes.** The 30 spike events per calibration frame are an assumption, above the expected real rate, chosen so that spike rejection is exercised on every frame. Real-frame recalibration replaces it.
- **Recalibration.** Users can recalibrate on their own Stripe-free frames at their own shape (`python -m denoisifier.recalibrate`, `calibrate_folder`) and pass the file to `analyse` or the CLI; evaluating on frames other than the Calibration Set is their job.
- **Gates.** `make check-slow` re-runs the calibration routine and requires every per-frame score to reproduce exactly; the held-out tests (false alarms at 1% and 5%, weak-stripe detection) run as `make check-held-out` at ticket 07's close and at `make measure` milestones only. A score-moving detector change re-stores the file through an approved procedure; an unexpected change is a finding.
- **Regions.** At the new, lower default more groups are reported as Striped Regions than at the provisional 154 of tickets 03-06, so region outputs at the default are not the ones ticket 05 evaluated.
- **Score steps.** The score can jump under small pixel changes (a group appears or vanishes at the size and leakage rules). The order statistic keeps its rate over frames whatever the per-frame continuity, but the score is a poor reading of "how close" a borderline frame was; a continuous score is a follow-up.
